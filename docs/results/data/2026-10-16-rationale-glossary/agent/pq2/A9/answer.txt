RATIONALE-STALE

The rule that governs this is R70: "`DELETE /v1/habits/{id}` answers 204, sets the habit's `deleted_at`, increments its `version`, and sets `deleted_at` on its non-deleted check-ins and nudges; nothing is removed from the database."

The owner did approve a rationale for R70, but it was written for an older wording of the rule. The rationale store marks it stale, and a stale note carries no usable text. I therefore can't tell you the owner's reason for keeping the rows, and I won't offer one as theirs. The only proposals on file are unapproved, and they are counted but never shown.

The spec does show how the soft delete works, though not why it was chosen:
- R70 marks the habit and its check-ins and nudges as deleted instead of removing them.
- The spec's intro list of assumptions (line 57, listed there as M12) says: "Deletes of habits and check-ins are soft (deleted_at, tombstone); deleting a habit tombstones its check-ins and nudges too. Account deletion is a hard delete."
- Line 189 of the spec says the sync response reports a soft-deleted row as a tombstone (`deleted` is true and `data` is null). Line 190 says a full sync includes tombstones. This suggests the tombstones exist so that sync can tell clients about deletions, but the spec doesn't say that is the reason.
- R26 contrasts this with `DELETE /v1/me`, which permanently deletes everything "(no tombstones)". R26 only has an unapproved rationale proposal.

Nothing was changed.
